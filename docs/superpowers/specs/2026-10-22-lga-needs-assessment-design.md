# LGA Needs Assessment — Design

**Date:** 2026-10-22 · **Status:** Draft for review

## Problem

Every analysis page works on one LGA at a time. Nothing ranks an LGA against the other 127, there is no composite need measure, and `/report` exports a screenshot PDF with no written findings. Planners need to pick one or several LGAs (or a whole region), see where need is concentrated, and lift the text and charts straight into business cases and reports.

## What we are building

1. **Needs engine.** A registry of indicators grouped into four domains. Every LGA gets a *need percentile* per indicator, computed against NSW, Greater Sydney and its region. Domain scores and an overall score roll these up, and each LGA gets a need band.
2. **Multi-LGA selection.** Choose one or more LGAs on `/needs` by search, by one-click region, or by clicking the map. The selection lives in the URL (`/needs?lgas=lga_blacktown,lga_penrith&mode=combined`) so a view can be shared and reproduced.
3. **Two views of a selection:**
   - **Separate (default):** each LGA keeps its own scores.
   - **Combined:** the selection is treated as one study area. Ratios are recalculated from summed counts. Non-additive indicators (SEIFA, commute distance) use a population-weighted mean and are labelled *approximate*.
4. **`/needs` page.** Charts: heatmap, strip plots, domain-score bars, radar, choropleth map, ranked table. Written findings are generated by rules from the data, and every sentence cites its source.
5. **Report-ready output:**
   - Every figure carries a numbered caption with its source, and can be exported as SVG, 3x PNG, a copied image or Excel data.
   - Every text block has a copy button.
   - The whole assessment exports to an editable Word document (.docx): headings, findings, native tables, and figures as high-resolution images.

## Indicators (v1)

Need direction: **↑** means a higher value is more need; **↓** means a lower value is more need.

| id | Domain | Label | Definition | Dir | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `car_dependency` | transport | Car dependency | (Car driver + car passenger) ÷ (100 − work from home) × 100, 2021 journey to work | ↑ | ABS Census 2021 G62 |
| `zero_car_households` | transport | Households without a car | noCar ÷ totalDwellings × 100 | ↑ | ABS Census 2021 G34 |
| `frequent_stop_share` | transport | Stops with frequent AM-peak service | `ptFrequency.frequentStopShare` | ↓ | TfNSW GTFS |
| `am_peak_departures_per_1000` | transport | AM-peak departures per 1,000 residents | `ptFrequency.departuresPer1000Residents` | ↓ | TfNSW GTFS |
| `commute_distance_km` | transport | Average commuting distance | `commuteDistance2016.avgKm` | ↑ | ABS Data by Region (Census 2016) |
| `crash_rate` | transport | Road crashes per 10,000 residents | latest-year totalCrashes ÷ ERP of that year × 10,000 | ↑ | TfNSW Centre for Road Safety |
| `seifa_irsd` | socio | SEIFA disadvantage (IRSD) | `seifaScore` | ↓ | ABS SEIFA 2021 |
| `unemployment_rate` | socio | Unemployment rate | `unemploymentRate` | ↑ | ABS Census 2021 G46 |
| `low_income_households` | socio | Low-income households | lowIncomeHouseholds ÷ Σ householdIncomeDistribution × 100 | ↑ | ABS Census 2021 G33 |
| `needs_assistance` | socio | People needing assistance | needsAssistance ÷ totalPopulation × 100 | ↑ | ABS Census 2021 G18 |
| `limited_english` | socio | Limited English proficiency | limitedEnglish ÷ totalPopulation × 100 | ↑ | ABS Census 2021 G13 |
| `older_residents` | socio | Residents aged 65+ | (65–74 + 75+ persons) ÷ totalPopulation × 100 | ↑ | ABS Census 2021 G01 |
| `indigenous_share` | socio | Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander residents | indigenousPopulation ÷ totalPopulation × 100 | ↑ | ABS Census 2021 G01 — **context only, not scored** (user decision 2026-10-22) |
| `pop_growth_2041` | growth | Population growth 2021–41 | (pop 2041 ÷ pop 2021 − 1) × 100 | ↑ | NSW DPE projections |
| `job_growth_2041` | growth | Job growth 2021–41 | (jobs 2041 ÷ jobs 2021 − 1) × 100 | ↑ | TfNSW TZP24 |
| `approvals_per_1000` | growth | Dwelling approvals per 1,000 residents (last 12 months) | rollingAnnualApprovals ÷ population × 1,000 | ↑ | ABS Building Approvals |

The four domains are **transport** (Transport disadvantage), **socio** (Socio-economic disadvantage), **growth** (Growth pressure) and **access** (reserved; not in v1). Safety sits within transport as `crash_rate`.

## Scoring rules

- **Need percentile:** mid-rank among LGAs with a value: `(countLess + 0.5 × countEqual) ÷ n × 100`, using "more need" order (direction applied). The result runs 0–100, where 100 is the highest need.
- **Universes:**
  - `nsw`: all 128 LGAs.
  - `gsyd`: `GREATER_SYDNEY_GCCSA_AREA_IDS`, 34 LGAs.
  - `region`: LGAs sharing `Area.region`.
  - `peer`: LGAs in the same peer group (see below).
  - A universe with n < 5 is marked `smallGroup`.
- **Peer groups.** Density = Census population ÷ boundary area (km²), with area computed from `public/geo/lga-nsw.json`. `metro` means the LGA is in `GREATER_SYDNEY_GCCSA_AREA_IDS`.

  | id | Label | Rule | ≈ n |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | `metro_inner` | Inner metropolitan | metro and density ≥ 4,000 | 7 |
  | `metro_middle` | Middle metropolitan | metro and 1,700 ≤ density < 4,000 | 13 |
  | `metro_outer` | Outer metropolitan | metro and density < 1,700 (includes Central Coast) | 14 |
  | `regional_city` | Regional city | not metro and population ≥ 50,000 | 19 |
  | `regional_town` | Regional town | not metro and 15,000 ≤ population < 50,000 | 23 |
  | `rural` | Rural and remote | not metro and population < 15,000 | 52 |

  Counts come from the 2026-10-22 cache. The original 3,000 / 1,000 thresholds were calibrated on the bundled integer `populationDensity`, which understates metro density by about half (Blacktown 784 vs 1,651/km²). They were recalibrated on real boundary-area density during Task 2.
- **Scored and context indicators.** Context indicators (`scored: false`, currently `indigenous_share`) still get a value and a percentile and appear everywhere, but they are excluded from domain scores, overall scores and key needs.
- **Weights are equal** (user decision 2026-10-22): every scored indicator within a domain, and every domain within the overall score. The Word export's data notes state this.
- **Domain score:** the mean of the scored need percentiles available in that domain. It is `null` when fewer than half of the domain's scored indicators have a value.
- **Overall score:** the mean of the non-null domain scores.
- **Word export:** plain default styling (Calibri 11, built-in heading styles), so it takes on the styles of whatever template it is pasted into.
- **Bands:** ≥ 80 Very high · ≥ 60 High · ≥ 40 Moderate · < 40 Lower.
- **Flags:** an indicator with a need percentile ≥ 80 is a *key need*.
- **Missing values** stay `null` all the way to the UI and the docx, shown as "No data". They are never treated as 0.
- **Combined selection:**
  - Ratio indicators aggregate as Σ numerator ÷ Σ denominator.
  - Non-additive indicators aggregate as a population-weighted mean with `approximate: true`.
  - The combined value's percentile is placed against the distribution of individual LGAs in the universe.
- **Benchmarks** (Greater Sydney, NSW) use the same aggregation over their universe, so there is one benchmark source.

## Written findings (narrative)

The narrative is deterministic: templates filled from values, percentiles and benchmarks, with no LLM involved. Sections:

- **Summary:** overall band and the top 3 key needs.
- **One paragraph per domain.**
- **Data notes:** coverage gaps, approximate values, and the 2021 Census caveat that Sydney was in lockdown, so work-from-home is inflated. Car dependency therefore excludes WFH.

Every sentence carries the citations of the indicators it uses.

## Insights layer (Part B)

Analysis beyond rankings, built from data already in the cache. The figures in this section come from a probe of `data/cache.db` on 2026-10-22.

1. **Expected car dependency (mismatch).** OLS of `car_dependency` on one predictor, fitted within the LGA's setting:
   - **Metro (34 LGAs):** predictor `frequent_stop_share`. R² 0.51, residual SD 8.8 pp.
   - **Non-metro (94 LGAs):** predictor ln(population). R² 0.52, residual SD 3.3 pp. Service level explains almost nothing outside Sydney (R² 0.02).
   - **Residual** = actual − expected. Flag it when |residual ÷ residual SD| ≥ 1.
   - **Show only when** n ≥ 20 and R² ≥ 0.3. Otherwise show: "The relationship in {setting} is too weak to estimate an expected value."
   - **The model is always displayed** with its R² and n.
2. **Trends.**
   - Car dependency (excluding WFH) for 2011 → 2016 → 2021. Coverage is 107 / 123 / 128 LGAs, because 2011 lacks the LGAs amalgamated in 2016.
   - Crashes per 10,000 residents, 2020 → 2024 (all 128), each year on its own ERP (the nearest ERP year, 2021, for 2020).
   - Population growth rate per year: actual from 2021 to the latest ERP year, against the DPE projection for 2021–2041. This answers "growing faster or slower than projected". The cached ERP covers only 2021–2025 for 123 LGAs, so a 2001–2021 window isn't available (R39).
   - Dwelling approvals in the last 12 months against the previous 12.
   - Each change is compared with the universe median change.
3. **Growth against service.**
   - AM-peak departures per 1,000 residents today (latest ERP), and in 2041 if service stays at today's level: `amPeakDepartures ÷ pop2041 × 1,000`.
   - The extra departures needed to hold today's rate: `amPeakDepartures × (pop2041 ÷ latest ERP − 1)`.
4. **Jobs–housing balance.** TZP24 jobs per resident, 2021 and 2041. Only 29 of 128 LGAs currently resolve TZP24 projections through `getEmploymentProjections`. Part B includes a data check, and the insight is shown only where data exists.
5. **Key findings.** Each insight gets a severity. The top 5 across the selection become a "Key findings" section at the top of the narrative and the Word document, and every finding is cited.

## Project-need figures (user request, 2026-10-23)

These figures show the case for transport projects in the selected areas, compared with the other LGAs.

**Need profile radar (5 axes).** It replaces the 3-axis "Domain profile" radar.
- Axes, in this order:
  1. Car dependency (`car_dependency`)
  2. Public transport service gap (`frequent_stop_share`; lower share = more need)
  3. Households without a car (`zero_car_households`)
  4. Socio-economic disadvantage (`seifa_irsd`)
  5. Population growth to 2041 (`pop_growth_2041`)
- Values are need percentiles (0–100) within the current comparison group. Raw values can't share axes across these units.
- Reference: a dashed ring at 50, labelled "Typical LGA ({universeLabel})". Add a Greater Sydney outline when the universe is `nsw` or `gsyd`. Benchmarks are always scored against NSW, so the outline would be inconsistent with `region` or `peer`.
- Up to 6 areas are drawn. A null percentile is plotted as a gap, never as 0.

**Quadrant scatters (2).**
- Points: every LGA in the current comparison group with both values (grey), with the selected areas coloured and the combined area as one point. The first `MAX_LABELLED` selected areas are labelled. Greater Sydney is marked when its value is available.
- Lines: dashed median lines at the comparison group's medians.
- Need quadrant: shaded and labelled where both x and y are on the "more need" side of their medians.
- The two charts:

  | id | x | y | Need quadrant label |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | `growth-service` | `pop_growth_2041` | `am_peak_departures_per_1000` | High growth, low service |
  | `disadvantage-service` | `seifa_irsd` (axis reversed so more disadvantage is to the right) | `am_peak_departures_per_1000` | High disadvantage, low service |

- The y axis is departures per 1,000 residents, not frequent-stop share, because 67 LGAs have a frequent-stop share of 0.

**Quadrant findings.** Each selected area in a need quadrant gets a cited insight (kind `quadrant`), which can appear in Key findings. Exact templates:
- "{Subject} is in the high-growth, low-service group: population growth to 2041 of {x} (median {mx}) and {y} AM-peak departures per 1,000 residents (median {my}) among {universeLabel}."
- "{Subject} is in the high-disadvantage, low-service group: a SEIFA IRSD score of {x} (median {mx}) and {y} AM-peak departures per 1,000 residents (median {my}) among {universeLabel}."

Severity is the mean of the two need percentiles ÷ 100.

Formatting:
- {x} and {mx} use `formatValue`, with its unit.
- {y} and {my} are bare numbers at the indicator's decimals.

## Out of scope (v1)

- LLM-written summaries.
- SA2 or suburb level.
- Editable charts inside Word (figures are images; text and tables are editable).
- Changing the header's single-area selector.
